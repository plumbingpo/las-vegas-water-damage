Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Las Vegas: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
What We Do

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Finished Basement Walls

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.
