Commercial Water Damage Prevention in Las Vegas, NV

Home / Blog / Commercial Water Damage Prevention in Las Vegas, NV

For owners and managers of commercial property in Las Vegas, NV, few risks cost as much as water damage. Restoration is only the direct cost, because a commercial loss also brings potential liability exposure, displaced employees, lost inventory, and interrupted business. It is much cheaper to prevent a loss than to react to one with restoration. Below are the most effective ways to keep water damage away from a commercial property in Las Vegas.

Start With Scheduled Building Maintenance

Water damage prevention rests on a thorough maintenance program for the building. Inspect the building envelope, fire suppression systems, HVAC equipment, plumbing, and roofing on a regular schedule. Keep written records of every repair and inspection, since they show due diligence, which can limit your liability and back up an insurance claim after a loss.

Leak Detection Technology

Early warning is the point of commercial leak detection, which alerts you before a leak does significant damage. Today's equipment is able to monitor supply lines, water heaters, places that tend to flood, and HVAC condensate lines. A few models will even cut the water supply on their own once they sense a leak. The investment in detection technology typically pays for itself many times over by preventing even one significant water damage event.

Emergency Preparedness

Plan ahead for a water emergency at your commercial property by listing emergency contacts for the restoration contractor (Las Vegas Restoration Co, (515) 203-8065), property management, and your insurance agent, training staff on the main water shutoff procedure, assigning employee roles and duties for a water event, setting procedures that protect inventory and critical documents, and agreeing on how to communicate with stakeholders and tenants.

Insurance Points to Check

Once a year, sit down with your agent and go over the commercial property policy. Ensure your coverage includes business interruption insurance, adequate building and contents coverage, and endorsements for equipment breakdown and backup of sewers and drains. Comprehensive coverage raises premiums by a relatively modest amount, so compare that with the cost of being underinsured.
