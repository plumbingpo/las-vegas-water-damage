Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Las Vegas

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. We serve Las Vegas homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Decayed Wall Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

Find Every Wet Area

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Open What Is Needed

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • The type of flooring and finishes involved, since some dry in place and others do not.
  • The location of the leak, because a wall or slab costs more to reach than an open cabinet.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.
